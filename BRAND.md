# Brand & Visual Direction · Silo Works

## Identity

- **Brand name:** Silo Works
- **Parent:** Shelterbelt, Experiment 002
- **Tagline:** *Might as well draw it out.*
- **Heritage signature:** Prairie Systems & Engineering
- **Internal ethos:** No ceremony, just a useful sketch. Grandpa Roger's farm-napkin clarity.

## Creative concept

A beautifully restored **midcentury North Dakota engineering manual** from a fictional prairie research operation, with hints of Cold War-era silo instrumentation. The history inspires the texture of the design, not a literal military simulation.

**70% engineering manual, 20% prairie farmstead, 10% Cold War detail.** These are creative proportions, not CSS specifications.

- Heritage: field drawings, workmanlike type, agrarian practicality, serial numbers, revision letters, technical labels.
- Whimsy: peculiar machine illustrations, gentle humor in empty states, surprising but restrained micro-details.
- Work surface: crisp, highly usable, professional; avoid weathering and noise directly beneath diagram content.

Use true near-black on paper where small text requires stronger contrast; never use rust/brass as tiny body text without verifying contrast. Color must not be the sole status cue.

## Typography

- **Wordmark:** bold, sturdy industrial grotesk/slab sensibility; create as text-first lockup before inventing bespoke lettering.
- **UI/body:** contemporary highly legible sans-serif; simple hierarchy, excellent numeric clarity.
- **Metadata:** restrained monospace for drawing number, revision, timestamps. Never turn the main interface into monospaced decoration.
- Use open-source/web-safe fonts with licenses appropriate for deployment; do not assume commercial font licensing.

## Graphic language

- Precision linework; tiny stamped revision marks; subtle drafting grid.
- Four node silhouettes with strong semantics: process rectangle, decision diamond, system module/cylinder-like rectangle, terminal pill/round shape.
- Selected state must be unmistakable but not neon.
- Connector style: orderly orthogonal or gently stepped lines, readable arrowheads, simple inline edge labels.
- Avoid excessive drop shadows, fake paper scratches on active canvas, radioactive symbols, weapons imagery, faux security classifications, and decorative panels taking workspace away.

## Application design

- The canvas is the hero and should look professional in meetings.
- Mildly warm paper, modern controls, precise alignment, minimal surface chrome.
- A printed drawing may include optional heading `FIELD DRAWING / REV. A` and a tasteful Shelterbelt credit. Exports should remain useful without branding.
- Keep Silo Works visually related to Grain Bin through thoughtful craft, restraint, and prairie heritage, **not** identical typography or a copied palette.
